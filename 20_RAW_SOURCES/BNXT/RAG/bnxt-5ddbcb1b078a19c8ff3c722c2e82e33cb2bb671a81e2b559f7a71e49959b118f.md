## Kit configurations

One system, four configurations. They differ mainly in the package content, and in the stroke of those sensors.

### How many suspension sensors

Which configuration you need depends on how many suspensions the vehicle has. Two suspensions — one front, one rear — is the base configuration; each additional suspension adds one sensor and one mounting kit. Everything else in the kit stays the same.

| Configuration | Suspension sensors | What is measured | Vehicle mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Base | 2 | Front suspension, rear suspension | MTB · MOTO |
| Base + 1 | 3 | Front left and right, rear | QUAD |
| Base + 2 | 4 | Front left and right, rear left and right | CAR |
| Custom | On request | Any combination, including non-BYB sensors | OPEN |

Vehicle modes are set in the device configuration — see [Using the unit](https://bybtech.it/manual/telemetry#using-the-unit).

The V3 has two sensor connectors and therefore covers the base configuration only. Three or four suspension sensors, or any extra sensor — brakes, speed, remote accelerometers — require a V3 Pro.

### Which stroke

Choose the sensor stroke from the travel of the suspension it measures, not from the vehicle it is fitted to. Four standard strokes cover almost every vehicle; anything else is made to order.

| Sensor | Standard strokes | Suits suspension travel up to | Body diameter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Long | 210 mm · 350 mm | up to 210 mm · 350 mm | 13 mm |
| Short | 80 mm · 150 mm | up to 80 mm · 150 mm | 9.5 mm |

Standard strokes. Strokes can be mixed as you like: fit whichever stroke each suspension needs, in any combination.

## Installation

### Suspension sensors

Each suspension sensor spans the moving and the fixed part of one suspension, held by a bracket at each end. Fit one sensor at a time.

1. Check the sensor stroke covers the full travel of the suspension it will measure.A sensor shorter than the travel is destroyed by the first hard hit — see [Kit configurations](https://bybtech.it/manual/telemetry#kit-configurations).
2. Choose a position where the sensor runs clear of the tyre, the linkage and anything that could strike it.
3. Fit one bracket to the moving part and one to the fixed part, using the mounting kit for that suspension.
4. Attach the sensor to both brackets through the self-aligning joints.In tight spaces, remove the upper rod-end joint and its grub screw to get the sensor in.
5. Set the sensor slightly retracted — 2 to 3 mm from fully extended — before you tighten anything.Never fit a sensor fully extended.
6. Cycle the suspension by hand through its full travel.The shaft must slide freely, without force and without twisting.The sensor must not reach the end of its own stroke before the suspension reaches the end of its travel.The carbon cover must not touch the frame, the chassis or the mountings.
7. Fit the carbon cover if it was removed, and calibrate it — see [Automatic sensor calibration](https://bybtech.it/manual/telemetry#unit-calibration).

### Digital brake sensors

V3 Pro only

The sensor reads a magnet on the brake lever. The sensing element sits at the end of the sensor opposite the cable, so that end is what the magnet must face.

1. Fit the sensor beside the brake lever with a thin cable tie, where it cannot interfere with the rider.Do not overtighten the cable tie — it may damage the sensor.
2. Fit the magnet to the lever with its south pole toward the sensor, so that it meets the end of the sensor face-on, sharing one axis with it.Never the cable end, never the flank of the body.The magnet carries no pole marking: with the unit on and the sensor connected, the correct side is the one that lights the sensor LED. If it stays off, turn the magnet 180° and refit.
3. Set the gap as small as the lever travel allows, without the magnet touching the sensor.With the lever released the LED is off; pulling the lever turns it on.

Brake sensor — magnet assembly

### Analog brake sensors

V3 Pro only

The analog sensors mount exactly like the digital ones and add an automatic calibration routine, so they report how hard the lever is pulled rather than only whether it is.

1. Fit sensor and magnet as for the digital sensors: beside the lever with a thin cable tie, the magnet south pole toward the sensor, facing the end opposite the cable and sharing one axis with it.Do not overtighten the cable tie — it may damage the sensor.The magnet carries no pole marking: with the unit on and the sensor connected, the correct side is the one that lights the sensor LED. If it stays dark through the whole lever travel, turn the magnet 180° and refit.
2. Set the gap as small as the lever travel allows, without the magnet touching the sensor.Correct installation: with the lever fully extended the LED is off, and it brightens gradually through the travel to full brightness with the lever fully pulled.

Brake sensor — magnet assembly

### Speed sensor

V3 Pro only

The speed sensor reads a magnet fitted to the wheel, once per revolution.

1. Fit the sensor with a cable tie to a fixed part of the suspension or chassis, close to the wheel.Do not overtighten the cable tie.
2. Fit the magnet to the wheel — spokes or disc carrier — with the south pole facing the sensor head.
3. Set both sensor and magnet as close to the hub as the parts allow, and the gap between them as small as possible.
4. Check detection before riding: with the unit powered on, turn the wheel.The sensor LED must light up each time the magnet passes. If it does not, close the gap or turn the magnet over.

Speed sensor — magnet assembly

## IMU orientation

How to read the acceleration and gyroscope data.

### Acquisition unit IMU orientation

The acquisition unit contains a 3-axis accelerometer and a 3-axis gyroscope. All acceleration and gyroscope channels appear in the software and follow the orientation shown below.

IMU axis orientation diagram

The axes belong to the unit, not to the vehicle. Mount the unit at any angle and calibrate: from then on the software rotates the readings so X, Y and Z line up with the vehicle instead of with the box.

### IMU calibration

V3 Pro only

IMU calibration teaches the system how the unit sits on the vehicle. It measures gravity in the unit's own axes while the vehicle stands still, works out the rotation between the two, and stores it. When you open a run, that rotation is applied to every acceleration and gyroscope channel, so the axes read as vehicle axes whatever angle the unit is mounted at.

This is why no bespoke mounting is needed. The unit does not have to sit level, square to the vehicle, or with Z pointing straight up — fit it wherever it is protected and out of the way, then calibrate.

1. Fit the unit in its final position and tighten the mounting.Any angle is allowed. Calibration is only valid for the position it was measured in.
2. Stand the vehicle on level ground as it sits when ridden, and leave it still.Hold it upright rather than leaning it: the calibration records the current position at the moment the calibration button is presses.
3. Press IMU calibration in the software or app.It takes a moment and needs no movement — the vehicle must not be rocked or pushed.
4. Choose whether Z reads positive up or positive down.A software setting, changeable at any time. Pick the convention your data analysis expects.

### Automatic sensor calibration

Recommended

Suspension sensors need no calibration: the system detects the maximum and minimum value of each suspension channel by itself. For accurate measurements, follow these rules.

* Install the linear sensors slightly retracted — never fully extended. Always retract the sensor by 2–3 mm.
* Make sure each sensor has enough travel to cover the full travel of the suspension it measures.
* Check that the carbon cover cannot collide with the frame or any mounting hardware.
* Enter the correct values in millimetres — suspension travel, sensor travel, wheel size and rear travel — in the vehicle configuration (Bike Config) menu of the app or PC software.
* Capture at least one moment per run with the suspension fully extended — a jump, or simply lifting the vehicle before the run starts.

### Hardware sensor calibration (expert users only)

V3 Pro only

The V3 Pro can also be calibrated at the unit. The procedure sets the minimum value of every sensor at once.

1. Install the linear sensors slightly retracted — 2–3 mm from fully extended.
2. Check that each sensor has enough travel to cover the full suspension travel.
3. Check that the carbon cover cannot collide with the frame or the mountings.
4. Enter the suspension travels, wheel size and rear travel in the vehicle configuration (Bike Config) menu.
5. With every sensor installed and the vehicle raised so the suspension hangs fully extended, press Calibrate.The current readings become the new minimum calibration values, and the app and software calculate from them.

| IMU + GPS | Both conditions are met at the same time. |

### BLE sensors — heart rate and power meter

The V3 Pro connects to commercial BLE heart rate monitors and cycling power meters. Each sensor is paired once; its unique ID is then stored in the device for future sessions.

1. Activate the sensor — wear the heart rate strap, or move the power meter.
2. Enable Wi-Fi mode on the BYB Telemetry.
3. Open the app or software and go to the device configuration menu.
4. Tap Scan heart rate sensor or Scan cycling power meter.
5. Wait for pairing to finish — the unique code of the paired sensor is displayed.
6. Set Heart rate sensor or Power meter sensor to YES before starting a run.

### BYB Chrono external triggers

BYB Chrono external trigger · pair

The V3 Pro records lap times from BYB Chrono external triggers placed along the track. Placement, pairing and the trigger’s own settings are in the [BYB Chrono manual](https://bybtech.it/manual/chrono#trigger).

Open lap
:   The track has a distinct start and finish; entry and exit points are separate.

Closed lap
:   The track is a loop with a shared start/finish line. The entry and exit laps are automatically excluded from calculations.

External trigger
:   Pairs Chrono with a trigger for accurate timing independent of GPS reception. Enable reception and declare the number of triggers used.

External trigger ID
:   The trigger name. Every trigger in use must carry the same ID — four triggers on one track all share it, for example "Trigger-TeamX". Choose a name of your own so it is not shared with another team on the same track. Default: Chrono.

Quick start — triggers

1. On the acquisition unit, set Lap type to match the track (closed or open circuit), set External trigger to Yes, and enter the External trigger ID and the number of triggers you will use.
2. Switch each trigger on and set its External trigger ID to the same name entered on the unit — all triggers in use carry one name, for example "Trigger-TeamX".
3. Place the triggers along the track as described in [Trigger placement on the track](https://bybtech.it/manual/telemetry#v3pro-trigger-placement), switched on, and check that each LED is blinking: a blinking LED means the trigger is active.

| --- | --- |
| Hold 8 s | Factory reset — restores the trigger to its default settings. |
| Hold 15 s | Forces a restart. |

External trigger button commands. Times are counted from the moment you press.

### Trigger placement on the track

Where the triggers sit decides whether the lap times are usable. A trigger too close to the start or the stop point fires while the vehicle is still stationary; two triggers too close together interfere with each other. Set the lap type first, then place the triggers to match it.

Open lap
:   At least two triggers: one about 50 m after the start point, one about 50 m before the stop point. Splits go in between — four triggers give start, two splits and finish.

Closed lap
:   One trigger at the start/finish line is enough — place it on a clear stretch, as far as possible from intersections, so a pass on another part of the loop cannot cross it. Add splits around the loop to break the lap down.

Intermediate splits
:   Extra triggers between the first and the last one, more than 100 m from each other. Declare the total number in use in the device configuration.

Order
:   Times are read in the order the triggers are crossed, so cross them along the track in that sequence — 1 first, then 2, 3 and 4 — and keep the same layout between runs if you want to compare splits.

Closed lap

Open lap

Four triggers in both cases, numbered in the order they are crossed. Closed lap: 1 at the start/finish line, 2 to 4 as splits. Open lap: 1 after the start, 2 and 3 as splits, 4 before the finish.

## Riders, vehicles and setups

### Rider, vehicle, device

All setup data is stored in the software. The unit itself holds only the name of the vehicle it is fitted to, plus the few values it needs while recording. Note that three separate things in the system are called a profile — the rest of this chapter assumes you can tell them apart:

Profile (user profile)
:   Who is riding — name, date of birth, weight and height. Runs and the Garage are filed under it. One profile is active at a time; switch it from the profile switcher on the Home page.

Vehicle, in the Garage
:   One vehicle — brand, model, configuration, year, geometry and its setup history. Created in the app or PC software; vehicles are not stored in the unit.

Profile, in device configuration
:   The profile and vehicle names the unit writes into every run. They are what lets a downloaded run match itself to the right profile and vehicle.

### Vehicle and rider configuration

Create the vehicle in the Garage with "+ Add vehicle". Brand and model are mandatory: the unit carries the name they make into every run, and the software matches that name back to this vehicle. Then enter the geometry the software needs to turn sensor movement into suspension travel, and the leverage ratio for each wheel.

Before every session (day or vehicle change)

1. In the Garage, select the vehicle you are about to ride.A green readiness dot means the required geometry is complete. Amber means a value is missing and the run will not load, asking you to fill in the missing details every time.

### How runs find their vehicle

Runs are matched by name, then by date. As each run is imported, the rider name it carries sends it to a rider profile, the device profile name matches it to a vehicle in the Garage, and the recording time selects the setup that was current at that moment. The run then keeps its own copy of that setup.

Importing never gets in your way:

* It never stops to ask you a question — every run is imported.
* A run whose rider is unknown goes into the Guest profile, and you can move it to the right rider later.
* A run whose name matches no vehicle is imported without one. No vehicle is created for it.
* Renaming a vehicle keeps the old name as an alias, so recordings that still carry it keep matching.

### When you change something mid-session

You moved a clicker between runs and typed it into the run notes/setup. On save, the software asks what that change means, because only you know. Three options:

Log it as a new setup
:   A new setup is added, dated at the start of this run, and this run is tied to it. Every later run uses it too.

Update the current setup
:   The vehicle did not change — the saved setup was wrong: a mistyped clicker, a value never filled in. It is corrected in place, with no new entry and no new date. Offered only when the run follows a saved setup.

Only this run
:   A one-off change that you undid afterwards. The run is marked "Custom" and the setup history is left alone, but the link to the vehicle stays, so the run still appears in that vehicle's filters and trends.

### Fixing a run afterwards

Nothing repairs itself, by design — the corrections are yours to make, and they are all in the run row or the run notes.

| Problem | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Wrong or missing vehicle | Run row, "Assign vehicle…". Several at once: select the runs, then "Assign vehicle". |
| Wrong setup | Run row, "Assign specific setup…", then pick from that vehicle's setups, newest first. It overwrites the run's own copy of the values. |
| The run predates every setup | Correct the history, not the run: edit the date of the setup, or add a new one, then assign it. |
| There genuinely was no setup | "No setup for this run". The run stops asking. |
| A change was never written down | Add the setup by hand, dated when the change was made, so the history has no gap. |

## Troubleshooting

Find the symptom, apply the fix. Anything not here: info@bybtech.it.

### The unit will not get a GPS fix

Switch the unit on a few minutes before the first run and leave it still, with the antenna in the open and a clear view of the sky. A fix normally takes about 5 minutes from cold. The run LED turns solid green when a reliable 3D fix is acquired.

If it still will not fix:

* Check the antenna cable is fully seated in the SMA connector.
* Move the antenna higher and away from metal, carbon and bodywork that shadow the sky.
* Move away from buildings, trees, awnings and paddock structures.

### The run has no GPS map

Data logging started before a fix was acquired, so the run holds sensor data but no position. Always wait for the run LED to turn solid green before you press run — red means the GPS is still searching.

### The unit is not responding

Hold the power button for about 15 seconds. The unit restarts on its own; nothing recorded is lost.

### The Wi-Fi password is not accepted

* Try the default password: bybtelemetry.
* If the password was changed and cannot be remembered, hold the power button for 8 seconds to factory reset the unit, then connect again with the default password.

### The unit does not appear in the Wi-Fi list

* Hold the run button for 2 seconds to enable connection mode — the run LED blinks fast blue when it is active.
* Look for BYB- followed by the device name, not BYB-Telemetry, if the unit was renamed.
* Turn mobile data off, or tell the phone to stay on this network: with no internet, phones often drop the connection on their own.
* Charge the unit — a red power LED means the battery is below 40%.

### A channel reads zero or a flat line

* Check the connector is fully seated and the cap is off the channel in use.
* Check the selected mode matches the vehicle and the sensors you have connected — see [Using the unit](https://bybtech.it/manual/telemetry#using-the-unit).
* Check the sensor cable for a pinch or a pulled connector at the sensor end.
* Swap the sensor to another channel: if the fault follows the sensor, the sensor is at fault; if it stays with the channel, the unit is.

On a unit with user-defined channels:

* Check each channel is mapped to the sensor actually plugged into it.
* Check the mapping was sent to the unit before the session — it cannot be changed afterwards.

### The suspension data looks wrong or clipped

A trace that never reaches full travel, or that flattens at the top or bottom, is nearly always calibration or fitment rather than a fault.

* Recalibrate: manual calibration must be repeated every time a sensor or a mounting is moved or refitted.
* Check the travel values entered for the vehicle match the suspension actually fitted.
* Check the sensor stroke covers the full travel of the suspension — see [Kit configurations](https://bybtech.it/manual/telemetry#kit-configurations).
* Check the mountings are not moving: a bracket that shifts under load flattens or distorts the trace.
* Check the sensor is not bottoming out before the suspension does, and that the joints are aligned so nothing binds.

### A run is missing, or the software will not load it

* Check the mandatory vehicle parameters are filled in — without them the software cannot load the run, and asks for the missing details every time.
* Check the run was stopped with the run button before the unit was switched off.
* Check the auto pause settings: with a minimum speed set, a session spent stationary records nothing.
* Reconnect and refresh the device panel — see [The Device panel](https://bybtech.it/manual/telemetry#pc-software).

### No brake or speed signal

V3 Pro only

* Check the gap between magnet and sensor end — a few millimetres, no more.
* Check the south pole of the magnet faces the end of the sensor, and that neither has moved.
* Check the channel mapping matches where the sensors are plugged in.

### The battery does not last a session

* Check the charge LED went off before you unplugged: it stays lit until the battery is full — see [Charging the system](https://bybtech.it/manual/telemetry#charging).
* Check the charger rating and charge time against [Specifications](https://bybtech.it/manual/telemetry#specifications).
