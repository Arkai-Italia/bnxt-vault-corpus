## Why guessing fails

Modern suspension is powerful, and complex. Every setup change is a guess until something measures it.

* ### You cannot feel one click

One click of compression is a small change. After a run, on different dirt, with tired legs, “it felt better” is not evidence that it was.
* ### You cannot remember last season

The setup that worked at that track in winter or last summer is gone unless something wrote it down.
* ### You cannot separate the rider/driver from the vehicle

A quicker run might be a better setup, or a better line. The recording tells you which.
* ### You cannot hand over a feeling

A rider says the front is harsh. A technician needs to know where in the travel, and how fast — and that is a sentence no one can hand over.

## What BYB Telemetry measures

More than six kinds of measurement, recorded together and time-aligned on every vehicle the platform fits, so every trace can be read against every other.

* ### Suspension and wheel travel

Suspension position at every corner, for the whole run, in millimetres and as a percentage of the travel available.
* ### Suspension and wheel speed and acceleration

How fast the suspension moves through its stroke, and how sharply that speed changes. It reaches 10000 mm/s on rough ground.
* ### Brake sensors

Fitted without opening the hydraulic circuit. Digital records clean on-off timing; analog records how hard.
* ### Speed sensors

Measured at the wheel rather than derived from GPS, which matters wherever the sky is not clear — under trees, in a valley, inside a car.
* ### GPS and lap timing

Position for the run, so a trace can be read against the piece of ground that produced it.
* ### Impact detection and orientation

A built-in IMU: 3-axis acceleration and 3-axis rotation. Impacts and landings show up here first. Remote accelerometers fit as extra sensors.

Why the sampling rate matters

## Fast enough to catch the event, not just its average

Suspension hits 10000 mm/s on rough ground. That decides how often a sensor has to look.

A sensor reading 50 times a second waits 20 ms between looks. At peak speed the shaft covers 200 mm in that gap, so what comes back is the average of the event rather than the event.

Off-road is the demanding case, not the forgiving one. Rocks, roots and braking bumps ask for short violent movements — and the shorter the impact, the faster a sensor has to be to see it happen at all.

## Questions about suspension data acquisition

What is suspension data acquisition, and why use it?
:   [Suspension data acquisition](https://bybtech.it/learn/what-is-data-acquisition-in-mountain-biking) measures how a suspension actually moves, then turns that motion into charts you can compare run to run. It replaces “it felt better” with a difference you can point at.

Do I need to be a data engineer to use it?
:   No. The workflow is record a baseline, change one setting, compare the two runs. Auto tuning proposes where to go next, and BYB Tech runs an online course for riders and technicians who want the whole method quickly.

How long does installation take?
:   Typically under 15 minutes, and quicker once you have done it before. The mounts are universal and fit both air and spring suspension. They fit the majority of suspensions and frames.

Do I have to calibrate the sensors?
:   No. BYB Telemetry zeroes itself automatically while you ride, so there is no calibration routine to run before a session. Telemetry V3 Pro also lets you set a zero manually, if you want to fix the reference yourself.

What is the difference between Telemetry V3 and V3 Pro?
:   Telemetry V3 records 2 analog channels at 500 Hz and fits mountain bike and motocross. Telemetry V3 Pro records 5 customisable channels at 1000 Hz, adds quad, car and OPEN mode, and exports raw data. Both use the same sensors.

Will it fit my bike?
:   The mounts are universal and work with air and spring suspension across mountain bike and motocross. Sensor strokes differ by vehicle — 210 mm and 80 mm for a mountain bike, 350 mm and 150 mm for motocross. Sensor length can be customised on request.

Do I need the brake and speed sensors?
:   They are optional. Add brake sensors when you want to know exactly where a rider was braking, and a speed sensor to track wheel speed directly and catch the moments a wheel locks.

Is this only for race teams and professionals?
:   No. Telemetry V3 exists for riders who want a repeatable setup process. V3 Pro is the tier built for teams, technicians, racers and anyone who wants to go deep into a setup.

Can I export the raw data as CSV?
:   Telemetry V3 Pro exports raw data as CSV. Telemetry V3 does not support CSV export. V3 Pro also exports raw data recorded in OPEN mode, including data from custom sensors.
