# Pre-Season Motorcycle Maintenance Checklist | Vojo

A motorcycle that has been stored needs more than a charged battery and fresh fuel. Time can change pressure, fluid condition, seals, electrical connections, and exposed metal even when the odometer does not move. Rodents, corrosion, leaks, and earlier damage can also remain hidden until the bike is disturbed.

This checklist uses the Motorcycle Safety Foundation's T-CLOCS structure. It is a memory aid, not a specification sheet: use the owner's manual for pressures, fluid types, adjustment methods, wear limits, fastener torque, and the procedure for returning your exact motorcycle from storage.

## [Before turning the key](https://vojomoto.com/blog/pre-season-maintenance-checklist#before-turning-the-key)

* Find the owner or service manual for the exact model and year.
* Review how the motorcycle was prepared for storage and what work was deferred.
* Check the floor and motorcycle for fuel, oil, coolant, brake-fluid, or suspension-fluid leaks.
* Inspect wiring, intake and exhaust openings, and the airbox area for damage or nesting material.
* Confirm the battery case and cables are not swollen, cracked, loose, or corroded.
* If fuel condition or storage procedure is uncertain, do not guess—follow the manufacturer guidance or use a workshop.

## [T — Tires and wheels](https://vojomoto.com/blog/pre-season-maintenance-checklist#t-tires-and-wheels)

* Measure pressure cold and set it for the specified load.
* Inspect tread, cuts, cracking, bulges, punctures, and unusual wear.
* Check rims or spokes, valve stems, bearings, and visible fasteners.
* Confirm each brake can hold the motorcycle and inspect pads, discs, and visible leakage.

Do not use a generic pressure or tread figure from the internet. The motorcycle label/manual and applicable local law control the requirement.

## [C — Controls](https://vojomoto.com/blog/pre-season-maintenance-checklist#c-controls)

* Move the handlebars through their full range and check for binding or cable tension.
* Check levers, pedals, pivots, cables, hoses, and routing.
* Confirm the throttle moves freely and closes as specified.
* Verify clutch and brake free play or hydraulic operation using the manual.

## [L — Lights and electrics](https://vojomoto.com/blog/pre-season-maintenance-checklist#l-lights-and-electrics)

* Inspect and secure battery terminals before energizing the system.
* Test low and high beam, tail light, both brake-light switches, indicators, horn, and required warning lights.
* Look for damaged insulation, pinched wiring, loose connectors, and corrosion.
* Confirm the engine stop switch and side-stand or other interlocks operate as documented.

## [O — Oil and other fluids](https://vojomoto.com/blog/pre-season-maintenance-checklist#o-oil-and-other-fluids)

* Check engine oil using the exact hot/cold, stand, and level-ground procedure.
* Check coolant only when safe and as the manual describes.
* Inspect brake and clutch reservoirs, hoses, master cylinders, and calipers for level and leakage.
* Check fuel lines, tank area, and connections before starting.
* Review time-based replacement requirements; low mileage does not make old fluid new.

Use only the specified fluid. The [motorcycle oil guide](https://vojomoto.com/blog/motorcycle-oil-guide-10w40-vs-5w40) explains viscosity, API, JASO, and additive labels.

## [C — Chassis and final drive](https://vojomoto.com/blog/pre-season-maintenance-checklist#c-chassis-and-final-drive)

* Inspect the frame, steering, swingarm, suspension, and visible fasteners for damage or play.
* Check fork and shock surfaces for leaks.
* For a chain, inspect slack at several points, lubrication, links, sprockets, guides, and sliders.
* For a belt or shaft, follow its distinct inspection and service procedure.
* Confirm luggage and accessories are secure and do not interfere with controls or suspension.

## [S — Stands](https://vojomoto.com/blog/pre-season-maintenance-checklist#s-stands)

* Inspect the side and center stands for damage.
* Confirm springs and retention work and the stand returns or stays in the specified position.
* Test any stand interlock exactly as the manual directs.

## [First start and controlled test](https://vojomoto.com/blog/pre-season-maintenance-checklist#first-start-and-controlled-test)

Start only after the static inspection is complete and the area is ventilated. Watch warning lights and listen for abnormal operation without placing hands near moving or hot parts. Stop immediately for fuel leakage, oil-pressure warnings, overheating, smoke, severe noise, or unstable running.

Before normal traffic, perform a short, low-speed test in a safe area. Confirm steering, clutch, throttle, gear selection, suspension, and both brakes. Recheck for leakage and loose parts afterward.

## [Treat storage history as part of the inspection](https://vojomoto.com/blog/pre-season-maintenance-checklist#treat-storage-history-as-part-of-the-inspection)

The same motorcycle can need a different return-to-service plan depending on how it was stored. Record the storage duration, temperature exposure, battery arrangement, fuel treatment, whether it was covered, and any maintenance performed before layup. Note known leaks, pest activity, water exposure, or the reason the motorcycle was parked. These details guide attention; they do not replace the model-specific procedure.

If the history is unknown, use that uncertainty as a reason for a more deliberate inspection. Do not assume low mileage means fluids, rubber, electrical connections, or fuel-system components are ready. Time continues to affect a motorcycle that is not being ridden.

| Storage observation | What to verify before riding | Conservative response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Battery was disconnected or maintained | Correct installation, terminal security, charging guidance, warning lamps | Stop if condition or charging behavior is uncertain |
| Motorcycle sat with fuel | Manufacturer restart guidance, leaks, odor, starting behavior | Do not continue with an active leak or abnormal running |
| Tires lost pressure | Cold specification, visible condition, valves and wheels | Get professional assessment for damage or persistent loss |
| Evidence of moisture or pests | Wiring, intake, nesting material, corrosion, blocked openings | Correct contamination or damage before starting |
| Previous fault or unfinished repair | What was diagnosed, replaced, and verified | Treat the repair as incomplete until evidence confirms otherwise |

## [Make the first start a controlled checkpoint](https://vojomoto.com/blog/pre-season-maintenance-checklist#make-the-first-start-a-controlled-checkpoint)

Before starting, move the motorcycle to a ventilated area and confirm that tools, covers, plugs, chargers, and storage materials have been removed. Keep people clear and know how you will stop the engine. Do not start simply to “see what happens” when fuel, oil, coolant, wiring, controls, or the intake remain questionable.

Observe instruments and warning lamps according to the owner’s manual. Listen and look without reaching near hot or moving parts. If operation is abnormal, shut down and preserve the observation instead of repeatedly restarting. A short recording from a safe position may help a technician, but it should never require approaching a moving component.

## [Recheck after heat and movement](https://vojomoto.com/blog/pre-season-maintenance-checklist#recheck-after-heat-and-movement)

Some concerns appear only after the motorcycle has warmed or moved. After the controlled test, park safely, switch off, and inspect again for leaks, loose items, abnormal odor, unusual heat, pressure loss, and changes in control feel. Let components cool before touching or opening anything the manual identifies as hot or pressurized.

Choose a short first route with an easy return and predictable conditions. Delay the ride if weather, traffic, darkness, fatigue, or unresolved maintenance would reduce your margin. The goal is to verify a reliable baseline, not to complete a planned distance.

Create a return-to-service record with the date, mileage, checklist result, pressure readings where appropriate, battery or fluid work, defects, repairs, and remaining tasks. If a workshop performs the work, ask for the inspected items and measurements rather than only a generic “seasonal service” label.

## [Stage unresolved work instead of hiding it](https://vojomoto.com/blog/pre-season-maintenance-checklist#stage-unresolved-work-instead-of-hiding-it)

Not every finding can be corrected during one inspection. Create a separate task for each unresolved item, assign a no-ride or monitor status based on the manual and qualified advice, and record the evidence needed to close it. Do not bury a brake, tire, fuel, steering, control, wheel, or structural concern inside a generic seasonal note.

Parts availability is not evidence that the motorcycle is safe to use while waiting. If a required repair is delayed, store the motorcycle securely and preserve the inspection state. When the part arrives, verify fitment and service requirements for the exact model rather than assuming that a catalog description is sufficient.

Before the first group ride or trip, complete a normal pre-ride check again. The return-to-service inspection establishes a baseline but does not replace the check immediately before departure. Confirm documents, weather, route, rider condition, and emergency arrangements as well as the motorcycle.

At the end of the first several rides, review whether pressure, fluid level, fastener security, starting behavior, charging, controls, or leaks have changed. Use the intervals and procedures specified by the manufacturer or technician. A stable follow-up closes the seasonal transition more reliably than one successful start in the garage.

## Frequently asked questions

Should I start a stored motorcycle before inspecting it?

Inspect for leaks, damaged wiring, nesting material, fuel-system problems, fluid levels, and other hazards first. Follow the manufacturer's storage-return procedure.

What does T-CLOCS stand for?

Tires and wheels, Controls, Lights and electrics, Oil and other fluids, Chassis, and Stands. It is a Motorcycle Safety Foundation inspection framework.

Is tire age alone enough to decide replacement?

No single generic age rule replaces manufacturer guidance and professional inspection. Check condition, damage, wear, pressure, production information, and the tire or motorcycle manufacturer's recommendations.

When is a professional inspection appropriate?

Use a qualified mechanic when storage history is unknown, safety-critical damage is suspected, required work exceeds your tools or skills, or you cannot verify correct operation.
