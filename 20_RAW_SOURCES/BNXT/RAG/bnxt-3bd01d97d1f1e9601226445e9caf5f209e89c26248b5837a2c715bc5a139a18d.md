# Pro bike: Zachary Pichon’s 2026 TM 300FI EN – the fastest bike in the GP paddock that isn't orange

Published on August 23rd, 2026 by Nye Davis

#### **Enduro21 takes an in-depth look at the weapon of choice for this year's E2 runner-up Zachary Pichon as the Grand Prix season draws to a close.**

As the grand finale of the 2026 EnduroGP was getting underway in Wales, we took a moment to catch up with the TM Moto Boano Factory Team and, would you believe it, Zach Pichon's bike happened to be sat ready for the weekend-opening Super Test – in such circumstances, it'd be rude not to ask a question or two about his set-up.

A whistle-stop tour of the bike with the TM team highlighted a handful of interesting details, largely reinforcing that, as well as out-of-this-world talent, mind-boggling fitness and, of course, a sizeable set of balls (metaphorically so for the girls), the world's best riders have an incredible feel for a bike that separates them from the rest of us. While the flying Frenchman's 300 four-stroke isn't wildly dissimilar to the stock TM you or I can buy, there are a handful of millimetre-precise tweaks going on here – and we really are talking millimetres.

### **Stretched swingarm**

Starting off with one of the main standout alterations is Pichon’s elongated swingarm set-up. We're only talking an extra 10mm, nothing particularly visible, but the different design, which adds a degree of extra stability – we're told – is clearly enough to make a noticeable difference and the rest of the team – Cavallo, Elgari, and Verzeroli – all prefer the longer unit over the stocker, too.

As far as suspension goes, Pichon runs a standard linkage and shock (the latter obviously tuned to his needs), and the forks, which are mounted through CNC-machined triple clamps, are those found in the 'factory fitted options' selection TM offers its customers – namely the twin-chamber KYB 'Special' units with DLC-coated stanchions. It’s a minor detail, but throughout the whole season, a pair of fork socks have been used to keep stanchions clean and seals healthy, even on bone-dry days.

Despite being one of the fastest guys between the tapes, we’re also told Zach runs his forks 'super soft' to get the bike pitched on its nose for an extra-quick turn-in. We didn’t get the specific details on what spring rates and settings he uses exactly, but given the common theory that faster rider equals stiffer set-up, the information is interesting enough to note.

Braking is handled by Brembo kit up front and Nissin at the back, as is la norme on TMs. Up front, a floating Ferodo disc deals with almost all of the former junior world champion’s braking input, as he apparently almost never touches the rear brake – so much so that a single set of pads will last almost the full season.

### **Enduro motor, MX exhaust**

The motor itself comes straight from the TM factory and is essentially bog standard, barring a spot of cylinder-head polishing. To unlock a few extra ponies, Zach runs a motocross silencer borrowed from the 250 MX (you'll notice the lack of EU homologation marking) and, as for keeping the bike cool, Boano silicone coolant hoses and a radiator fan have been installed.

On a similar note – and one that we almost missed – Pichon runs a clutch master cylinder reservoir extender (look closely and you’ll spot it) to allow for an increased fluid volume, helping to prevent fade by preventing fluid overheating.

### **Sneaky tweaks**

Remember those millimetre-scale changes we mentioned earlier? Well, although the TM guys wouldn't let us photograph them, Pichon was running the old-style radiator shrouds in Wales rather than the latest design. The reason? The reworked plastics are approximately 2mm wider around the airbox on each side.

The new shrouds are easily identified by several holes drilled near the top, designed to improve airflow to a rejigged airbox.

### **Boano hard parts**

There's a healthy smattering of Boano kit dotted around Zach's TM.

We've mentioned the coolant hoses above, but there's also a plastic rear disc guard, chain guide and a full-length Cross Pro sump guard, extended back to keep the linkage out of harm's way.

Boano also supply the Racing Steel footpegs, while the rest of his control set-up consists of a ribbed seat cover from OneGripper and the OEM TM-sweep Reikon bars, which, unlike that Spanish chap Garcia’s KTM (read about his 250 EXC-F here), are set all very conventionally, right in line with the fork angle.
