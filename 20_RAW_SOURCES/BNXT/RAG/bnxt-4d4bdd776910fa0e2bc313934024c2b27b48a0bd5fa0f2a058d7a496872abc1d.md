    OPEN Structural optimization principles
           for edge AI in motorsport telemetry
                     Rubén Juárez Cádiz1 & Fernando Rodríguez-Sela2

                               In motorcycle motorsport, engineering decisions are constrained by mass, stiffness, fatigue life, and
                          thermal margins. In a comparable way, deploying large language models (LLMs) for edge or trackside
                             assistance is limited by memory capacity, bandwidth, latency, and power. This paper presents a
                           systems-engineering framework that relates structural lightweighting and post-training quantization
                          (PTQ) through second-order sensitivity. In mechanics, safe material removal is governed by the
                                stiffness matrix K; in neural networks, safe precision reduction is governed by curvature information
                        encoded in the loss Hessian H. To ground this analogy, we combine a structural case study based on
                          topology optimization of a Formula Student upright with an edge-oriented evaluation of low-precision
                    LLM deployment using established PTQ pipelines (GPTQ and AWQ). The structural example illustrates
                     how sensitivity-guided material removal preserves load paths and mechanical integrity, while the
                    LLM study examines how sensitivity-aware quantization can reduce computational footprint while
                           maintaining operational usefulness in telemetry-related inference. To assess deployment feasibility, we
                            define two practical metrics: a Digital Factor of Safety (FoSdig), based on a perplexity-derived integrity
                             threshold, and Intelligence-per-Watt (IPW), which captures energy-aware inference efficiency. For a
                            32B-class operating point, INT4 weight-only deployment reduces memory footprint from 61 GB to
                        18 GB, improves throughput from 26.0 to 69.9 tok/s, and lowers measured power from 295 W to 165 W
                           while remaining within the proposed integrity envelope. The contribution is methodological rather
                         than algorithmic: we do not introduce a new quantization method, but a reproducible framework for
                            analyzing when low-precision LLM deployment becomes feasible under motorsport-inspired resource
                              constraints. The results support the practical relevance of stiffness-informed digital lightweighting,
                           while also highlighting that the evidence is limited to the structural and computational case studies
                           considered here.

                       Modern racing motorcycles are high-velocity cyber-physical systems (CPS) in which rider, chassis, tires, and
                                 electronics form a tightly coupled dynamical loop. Unlike many four-wheel platforms, competitive motorcycles
                          must preserve stability at extreme lean angles under high vibration and tight packaging constraints, while the
                                rider remains both a time-varying mass and the dominant controller in the loop1,2. These conditions create
                          two concurrent engineering pressures: (i) reducing physical mass without compromising structural integrity,
                                     stiffness, or fatigue life, and (ii) reducing decision latency without destabilizing the broader supervisory loop.
                            The embedded environment is mechanically and thermally demanding. Motorsport ECUs and acquisition
                                units must tolerate sustained vibration, elevated internal temperatures, and sealed packaging that constrains
                           both power budgets and heat rejection3–5. At the same time, premier-class machines integrate dense sensing
                         and control layers—including IMU-based estimation, traction and wheelie control, ride-by-wire, and map
                         management—that generate high-rate telemetry streams whose interpretation often needs to occur within short
                              garage or post-run windows5. Because live air-to-ground telemetry is restricted in many competitive contexts,
                                useful engineering intelligence must often be generated close to the data, either at the edge or immediately after
                              the run6.
                            Edge AI is attractive in this setting because it can transform multi-channel telemetry into compact, actionable
                              engineering language. However, large language model (LLM) inference  is frequently memory-bound: in
                     common decoding regimes, the working set of weights dominates bandwidth and capacity, making the memory
                               wall a practical constraint for embedded deployment7–9. This constraint is the digital analogue of a motorsport

                     A systems-engineering correspondence. This paper examines a systems-engineering correspondence
                           between mechanical lightweighting and post-training quantization (PTQ). In structural mechanics, safe
                               material removal is guided by sensitivity information encoded in the stiffness matrix K. In neural networks, safe
                               precision reduction is guided by curvature or saliency information encoded in the loss Hessian H. On this basis,
                       we ask whether sensitivity-guided structural optimization can serve as an analytical lens for sensitivity-guided
                                quantization. The formal development of this correspondence is presented in Section 2.4.1, while Section 2.4.3
                               discusses the geometric interpretation of GPTQ as a nearest-plane approximation under a curvature-induced
                                  metric10–12. Figure 1 summarizes this conceptual bridge. Importantly, the framework is not intended to claim
                                 constitutive equivalence between the two domains, but rather to provide a disciplined comparison of how
                             second-order sensitivity governs resource removal under performance constraints.
                                 This type of sensitivity-guided design is already familiar in sports engineering. Prior studies have used
                          measured loads and telemetry-informed scenarios to optimize Formula Student uprights13 and suspension bell-
                                cranks14, showing that in high-performance environments, material removal is not heuristic but driven by load
                                paths, stiffness retention, and durability constraints. In the present work, we extend this logic from the physical
                                 chassis to the digital chassis, where the objective is not to remove material but to remove excessive numerical
                               precision while preserving operational usefulness.
                              At the same time, previous work in LLM compression has primarily emphasized quantization accuracy,
                             throughput, and memory reduction, whereas previous work in structural optimization has emphasized
                             mass, stiffness, and fatigue preservation. What remains less developed is a deployment-oriented framework
                                 that connects both domains through a shared second-order sensitivity logic and evaluates feasibility using
                               acceptance-style metrics relevant to motorsport workflows, such as integrity margin, power-aware efficiency,
                         and short decision-window usability. In this sense, the contribution of the present study is methodological rather
                            than algorithmic: we do not propose a new quantization method or a new structural optimizer, but a cross-
                         domain framework for analyzing when low-precision LLM deployment becomes feasible under motorsport-
                               inspired resource constraints.

                     The motorsport paradigm
                          The motorsport philosophy of “adding lightness” seeks to remove non-functional mass while preserving the
                               properties that govern controllability and durability, including stiffness, modal behavior, and fatigue  life.
                           Topology optimization methods such as BESO provide a formal route for retaining critical load paths while
                           removing low-contribution material13,15,16. Following Angeles17, the stiffness matrix can be interpreted as a
                             second-order energy object whose eigenstructure reveals mechanically soft directions, thereby motivating
                                sensitivity-guided removal under explicit integrity constraints. In this paper, that physical logic is grounded by a
                                 structural case study based on the topology optimization of a Formula Student upright.

                     The edge AI paradigm: hessian-aware quantization
                               Scaling laws suggest that model capability often increases with size, yet embedded feasibility remains constrained
                           by memory traffic, available capacity, and power envelopes8,18,19. Second-order and activation-aware PTQ
                           methods, such as GPTQ and AWQ, use curvature and importance signals to preserve function while discretizing
                               weights, which is conceptually analogous to preserving critical load paths while removing physical mass10,11.
                                Kernel-level realization is the implementation counterpart that determines whether bit-width reduction actually
                                  translates into practical throughput and latency gains under batch-1 decoding20.

                     The convergence hypothesis
                         To make this correspondence empirically examinable, the study is organized around four falsifiable hypotheses:
                      H1 (Isomorphism of sensitivity). If physical stiffness K acts as a second-order energy object and the loss
                            Hessian H governs error sensitivity, then sensitivity-guided PTQ should be structurally isomorphic—at the level
                                of design logic rather than physical mechanism—to sensitivity-guided topology optimization.
                      H2 (Geometric interpretation of GPTQ). If GPTQ can be interpreted as a nearest-plane lattice projection
                           under a metric induced by H, then it provides a more principled reconstruction rule for quantized weights than
                            magnitude-only heuristics.
                      H3 (Operational viability via INT4).  If inference remains memory-bound under motorsport-style
                        embedded constraints, then INT4-class weight-only pathways are expected to be required to satisfy feasibility
                             budgets while remaining within an acceptable inference-integrity envelope.
                      H4 (The edge window). Given telemetry restrictions and short decision windows, kernel-realized INT4
                                pipelines may enable practical semantic assistance close to the data within strict time budgets.

                       Paper organization
                          The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 develops the theoretical correspondence between
                                 structural lightweighting and post-training quantization, including the K ↔H sensitivity interpretation and                              the geometric view of GPTQ. Section 3 describes the structural and computational methodology, measurement
                                protocol, and deployment conditions. Section 4 presents the empirical results and hypothesis-oriented evaluation,
                              including the structural case study and the low-precision inference results. Section 5 discusses implications,
                                  limitations, generalizability, and threats to validity. Finally, Section 6 concludes the paper and outlines future
                            work.

                       Theoretical framework and cross-domain sensitivity mapping
                          The central premise of this paper is that digital lightweighting—that  is, LLM quantization for embedded
                             or edge deployment—can be examined using a systems-engineering logic that is comparable, at the level of
                              constrained design, to physical lightweighting in motorcycle motorsport. In both domains, feasibility depends
                        on function-preserving reduction under binding constraints: in mechanics, stiffness, modal margins, fatigue life,
                         and manufacturability; in inference, semantic integrity, latency, memory bandwidth, and power dissipation. The
                           purpose of this section is therefore not to claim a constitutive identity between structure and computation, but
                                to establish a common analytical language based on second-order sensitivity. In structural mechanics, this role
                                        is played by the stiffness matrix K; in optimization and quantization, it is played by the curvature matrix H21,22.
                            The framework is developed through two complementary arguments. First, we formalize a second-order
                            correspondence between K and H using potential-energy interpretations in mechanics and local quadratic
                             approximations in optimization17. Second, we interpret Hessian-aware quantization, particularly GPTQ, through
                             a geometric view as a nearest-plane approximation to a Closest Vector Problem (CVP) under a curvature-
                            induced metric10,12. This theoretical framing supports the paired structural and computational methodology
                             introduced in Section 3 and the operating-point analysis reported in Section 4.

                          Positioning relative to prior work and research gap
                               Existing work in structural lightweighting and in post-training quantization has matured along largely separate
                                   trajectories. In motorsport and structural optimization, emphasis is placed on mass reduction under explicit
                                     stiffness, stress, and fatigue constraints13–16. In LLM compression, the dominant focus is on preserving model
                                quality while reducing memory footprint and improving inference efficiency, as seen in methods such as LLM.
                                   int8, SmoothQuant, GPTQ, and AWQ10,11. What remains less developed is a deployment-oriented framework
                                 that connects both domains through a shared sensitivity logic and evaluates low-precision inference using
                               acceptance-style criteria relevant to motorsport operation: integrity margin, power-aware efficiency, and short
                            decision-window feasibility.
                                 Table 1 summarizes this positioning. The objective is not to argue that prior methods are insufficient in their
                                 original contexts, but to clarify the specific gap addressed here: a systems-engineering framework for judging
                       when aggressive low-precision LLM deployment becomes feasible under edge constraints inspired by motorsport
                              telemetry workflows. In addition, because Reviewer 1 requested stronger grounding on the structural side, the
                              present study explicitly complements the analytical mapping with a concrete structural case study, rather than
                                relying on the analogy alone.

                          Structural materials vs. numeric data types
                             In motorcycle motorsport, materials are not “best overall”; they are best under constraints, including stiffness-
                              to-weight ratio, fatigue resistance, manufacturability, and cost. A similar engineering logic applies to numeric
                             formats in computation. Floating-point and integer data types act as computational materials: they trade
                          dynamic range, precision, numerical stability, memory footprint, and hardware acceleration8,9. This choice
                                        is especially important for Transformer inference because autoregressive decoding is often bottlenecked by
                      memory movement rather than arithmetic throughput. In such regimes, shrinking the weight representation
                                  directly reduces bandwidth pressure7,9.

                                  directly governs (i) weight footprint and, therefore, (ii) the lower bound on weight traffic per generated token in

                          Implications for on-track “Edge Windows”
                               Premier-class motorcycle racing imposes a distinctive operational envelope: data are generated on-bike at high
                                     rate, while live air-to-ground telemetry is restricted, forcing decision support to be produced locally and/or
                             within short garage windows immediately after runs6. This context sharpens the practical meaning of Eq. (1): for

                             Quantization methods differ mainly in the measurement signal they use and in how they preserve structurally

                       Discussion
                       Cross-domain interpretation: one constraint logic, two substrates
                          The structural and digital branches of this study do not share identical physics, but they do share a common
                              systems-engineering logic: sensitivity-guided reduction preserves function while unlocking feasibility under a
                              binding constraint. In mechanics, topology optimization removes material while respecting stiffness, stress,
                             deformation, and fatigue-related constraints through sensitivity information encoded in system matrices15,16.
                             In the digital domain, INT4 post-training quantization (PTQ) removes “bit-mass” while preserving inference
                                  integrity through curvature- and activation-aware sensitivity10,11,25,26.
                            The correspondence established in Section 2 should therefore be interpreted as an analytical correspondence
                                 at the level of second-order design logic, not as a claim of constitutive identity. In both domains, a second-order
                              penalty is bounded under a hard resource budget: compliance or stored energy in the structural case, and loss
                               increase or reconstruction penalty in the digital case. This is the basis on which the K ↔H bridge becomes                                useful for systems engineering.
                            The results support this interpretation in a limited but concrete way. In the structural branch, mass is reduced
                          from 2.45 kg to 1.01 kg while the optimized upright remains inside the adopted stress and fatigue envelope. In
                              the digital branch, VRAM is reduced from 61 GB to 18 GB while the measured GPTQ-INT4 operating point

                         Discussion aligned with H1–H4
                    H1 (Isomorphism of sensitivity). H1 posits that the same second-order logic governs “safe removal” in both
                                  substrates. Mechanically, BESO and related topology-optimization methods use sensitivity information (e.g.,
                               strain-energy density) to preserve load paths while removing low-contribution mass (Eqs. 2–4). In the present
                                 structural case study, that logic becomes visible in the topology-evolution sequence: the final part is not simply
                                 smaller, but more explicitly organized around the dominant transmission paths between interfaces. Digitally,
                    PTQ uses curvature or saliency (Eq. 5) to preserve model function while discretizing weights. The contribution
                             here is not to claim identical constitutive laws, but to identify a comparable engineering control variable: a global
                             second-order sensitivity structure encoded by a system matrix (K or H) that guides removal decisions.
                      H2 (Geometric interpretation of GPTQ). H2 argues that GPTQ is principled because it implements a
                             curvature-weighted reconstruction rule rather than independent rounding. By rewriting the PTQ objective as
                             a metric projection (Eq. 16 together with the factorization H = R⊤R in Eq. 17), the GPTQ update admits a
                                nearest-plane/closest-vector interpretation under a Hessian-induced geometry12. Methodologically, this matters
                             because it clarifies why sensitivity-aware PTQ remains comparatively stable at low bit-width: reconstruction
                               error is redistributed along correlated directions rather than accumulated in high-curvature modes.
                      H3 (Operational viability via INT4). H3 is supported by the empirical operating-point shift reported
                               in Section 4: moving from BF16 to GPTQ-INT4 reduces memory footprint substantially while increasing
                               delivered throughput and reducing measured power (Tables 11 and 12; Fig. 12). This Pareto-improving behavior
                                        is consistent with memory-traffic-dominated decoding: reducing weight bytes relaxes the memory-wall lower
                        bound in Eq. 17–9. In the motorsport edge-window context, this is not cosmetic optimization: it materially affects
                           whether the system can remain interactive within short post-run time budgets.
                      H4 (The edge window). H4 is strengthened by expressing feasibility deterministically as a throughput-to-
                            time mapping (Eq. 19): given a token budget T and a measured throughput τ, the interaction time is bounded by
                                tgen ≈T/τ. This framing avoids speculative claims about “real-time” operation and ties the systems argument                                  directly to measurable quantities. The contribution is therefore not to claim closed-loop vehicle control by
                              the LLM, but to show that low-precision semantic assistance can fit within constrained post-run engineering
                             workflows.
                         Domain validity beyond perplexity. Perplexity serves as a broad statistical proxy for linguistic retention, but
                                             it does not by itself guarantee factual correctness in domain-specific reasoning. The TEE-20 results in Table 13
                              strengthen the interpretation of H2 by showing that domain-specific utility is preserved more robustly than

                          Structural progression and what can reasonably be generalized
                      One of the central requests from Reviewer 1 was to clarify whether the structural conclusions extend beyond a
                                 single example. The upright case study supports a useful but limited generalization.
                         What appears robust across structural configurations is not a specific final shape, but a pattern of constrained
                                reduction. Interfaces, bearing seats, and load-introduction zones tend to remain materially privileged because
                                 their local contribution to stiffness and load transfer is high. By contrast, low-energy internal regions are the first
                              candidates for removal. This is the structural analogue of preserving high-curvature or high-saliency weights
                            during aggressive quantization.
                              At the same time, the exact optimized morphology is configuration-dependent. Boundary conditions, load
                             combinations, manufacturing constraints, and packaging all influence the final topology. For that reason, the
                              present paper does not claim that the upright geometry is universally representative of all motorsport structural
                           components. Rather, the upright is used to demonstrate the design logic by which a sensitivity field governs
                          where reduction remains acceptable and where it does not. This is also why the generalizable claim of the paper
                                        is methodological rather than morphological.

                          Inference integrity envelope: Digital Factor of Safety as an acceptance gate
                            Mechanical lightweighting programs reduce mass while tracking an explicit margin to failure. Analogously, PTQ
                             reduces precision while approaching a semantic-failure boundary defined through unacceptable degradation
                               in perplexity or task utility. The Digital Factor of Safety introduced in Section 2.6 (Eq. 11) makes this integrity
                             envelope explicit: it quantifies the headroom between a measured operating point and a task-defined collapse
                                  threshold.
                    A key methodological point is that κ should be treated as application-calibrated rather than universal.
                              Accordingly, reporting the chosen κ and the rationale for the collapse criterion is part of reproducible reporting.
                           Within the present dataset, FoSdig is best understood as an acceptance-style deployment gate: it does not prove
                             semantic correctness, but it helps distinguish operating points that remain comfortably inside the chosen
                                  integrity envelope from those that approach unacceptable degradation.
                                 This is directly parallel to the use of explicit structural margins. Just as a positive fatigue margin does not
                             guarantee universal mechanical immunity under all future load cases, a positive FoSdig does not guarantee
                               universal semantic correctness across all future prompts. In both cases, the metric is most useful as an engineering
                             acceptance tool under stated assumptions.

                  Human latency budget and cognitive ergonomics
                  A common objection in motorsport systems engineering is that garage analysis does not require real-time
                           performance because the vehicle is stationary. The relevant distinction, however, is between control latency and
                                 interaction latency. The LLM is not placed inside a millisecond-scale stabilization loop; instead, it operates within
                              the human problem-solving loop of the engineer during a constrained post-run window.
                                 This difference is operationally important. Engineering diagnosis is rarely a single-shot query; it is typically
                          an iterative exchange:

                                    1.  Engineer: “Why is the rider reporting understeer in Sector 3?”
                                    2.  System: “Telemetry shows front fork bottoming out at the T9 braking marker.”
                                    3.  Engineer: “The fork is already at maximum stiffness. What is the rear pitch doing?”
                                    4.  System: “Rear rebound is fast, causing rapid weight transfer and front unloading.”

                         Under high-latency conditions, the interaction becomes cognitively expensive. Established HCI results show
                                 that delays above a few seconds begin to disrupt flow, and delays above roughly 10 seconds push users toward
                                attention loss or task switching34–36. In practical terms, a 30-second response regime encourages broad,
                                infrequent, high-confidence queries and discourages exploratory hypothesis testing. The model is then treated
                                as a static oracle rather than as an interactive reasoning aid.
                           By contrast, the INT4 operating point reported in Section 4.8 shifts the system toward a conversational
                              regime. At approximately 70 tok/s, a typical engineering response of 150–200 tokens can be delivered in roughly
                          2–3 s. This preserves the engineer’s chain of thought and supports iterative interrogation: the user can ask a
                                 tentative question, inspect the model’s intermediate logic, and immediately refine the next prompt. In this sense,
                           low latency is not a vanity metric; it is a usability constraint for pit-lane viability.

                       Motorsport embedded environment: ECU/DAQ constraints and supervisory compatibility
                         To keep the contribution grounded in motorsport systems engineering rather than in generic accelerator
                           benchmarking, we frame the deployment target as the race ECU/DAQ stack: a mechatronic subsystem operating

                               Telemetry as a physical signal chain. Motorsport DAQ stacks capture and curate high-rate sensor streams
                                 for analysis and decision support37,38. In the present interpretation, tensor flow through accelerator memory
                                hierarchies behaves analogously to an engineering signal chain: bottlenecks localize where effective cross-section
                            (bandwidth) is insufficient, consistent with the roofline and memory-wall framing8,9.
                                Supervisory-loop compatibility. The quantized LLM is not positioned inside inner, kilohertz-rate control
                                loops. Rather, it acts as a supervisory semantic layer operating on aggregated telemetry windows or post-run
                          dumps. Its outputs are structured advisories—diagnostic hypotheses, hazard annotations, and setup-oriented
                          recommendations—that can inform or bias existing deterministic control and analysis pipelines. This distinction
                                        is important because it bounds the claim of the paper: the LLM is compatible with supervisory engineering
                             workflows, not proposed here as a replacement for deterministic inner-loop control.
                      Why unified memory matters (platform-level implication). Automotive compute platforms increasingly
                           emphasize coherent shared-memory models to reduce copy overhead between CPU and accelerators. Such
                                 features directly support lower-jitter supervisory inference by keeping the telemetry-to-model data path compact
                         and predictable23. In the present framework, this matters because any reduction in unnecessary data movement
                            improves the practical viability of the edge window.

                         Closing insight
                             Across both substrates—metal and bits—the same engineering pattern recurs: naive removal fails, whereas
                                sensitivity-guided reduction preserves function under a strict budget. Under telemetry restrictions and short
                          edge-window timing, quantization is best interpreted not as post hoc compression but as structural optimization
                                   for deployment feasibility7,9–11,20.
                                  Accordingly, the present work should be read as a systems-engineering feasibility study with a cross-domain
                                 analytical framing and deployment-oriented metrics, rather than as a new PTQ algorithm or as a closed-loop
                             experimental validation of a race vehicle.

                      Conclusion and future work
                              This paper has presented, through a systems-engineering lens, the argument that aggressive low-precision
                           deployment of LLM-based assistance for motorcycle motorsport can be interpreted as a form of structural
                               optimization for deployment feasibility, rather than as a purely post hoc compression step. The proposed
                            convergence between topology optimization and post-training quantization (PTQ) provides a shared technical
                             language across substrates: stiffness and load-path sensitivity in mechanics, encoded by K, are compared with
                              curvature and importance sensitivity in neural networks, encoded by H21,22,25,26.
                               Across the structural branch, the Formula Student upright case study illustrates a sensitivity-guided
                               lightweighting process in which mass is reduced from 2.45 kg to 1.01 kg while the final validated response
                            remains within the adopted structural and fatigue envelope, with σVM,max = 107.52 MPa below the fatigue limit
                                of 159 MPa. Across the digital branch, the measured operating points show that moving from BF16 to GPTQ-
                       INT4 yields a Pareto-improving shift in feasibility for a 32B-class setting: VRAM decreases from 61 to 18 GB,

                      Hypotheses revisited (H1–H4)
                    H1 (Cross-substrate sensitivity isomorphism). The core convergence claim is that both lightweighting problems
                                are governed by a global second-order sensitivity structure. In mechanics, K encodes load-path sensitivity and
                              guides safe removal or preservation of material. In PTQ, H encodes loss curvature or importance and bounds
                                  safe discretization through a quadratic penalty (Eqs. 5 and 16). The support for H1 should be interpreted at the
                                   level of design logic, not as a claim of constitutive identity between the two domains.
                      H2 (Geometric interpretation of GPTQ under the Hessian metric). Hessian-aware PTQ improves over
                            independent rounding because  it minimizes reconstruction error in a curvature-induced geometry. The
                                 factorization H = R⊤R converts PTQ into a metric-projection/nearest-plane problem (Section 2.4.3; Eqs. 16–
                                      17)10,12. This provides a principled rationale for why sensitivity-aware PTQ remains comparatively stable at
                          INT4, and the TEE-20 results further indicate that this stability extends beyond perplexity into task-grounded
                              engineering reasoning.
                      H3 (Feasibility via bandwidth relief: VRAM↓ and throughput ↑). The measured operating points show a
                                  clear shift toward deployability: BF16 → GPTQ-INT4 reduces footprint (61 → 18 GB), increases throughput
                                (26.0 → 69.9 tok/s), and reduces power (295 → 165 W) (Tables 11 and 12; Fig. 12). This is consistent with                          memory-wall and roofline expectations for weight-dominant decoding (Eq. 1)7–9, while also showing that
                                 kernel-level realization is required for theoretical compression benefits to translate into batch-1 decode gains20.
                      H4 (Edge-window feasibility can be stated deterministically). With measured throughput τ  (tok/s),
                             response time scales as tgen ≈T/τ for a T-token generation (Eq. 19). At the GPTQ-INT4 operating point, this                                 yields concrete post-run interaction times inside the garage decision window, avoiding vague claims about speed
                             or usability. The result supports supervisory semantic assistance within short engineering windows; it does not
                            imply real-time closed-loop vehicle control by the LLM.

                          Inference integrity envelope and IPW as embedded design constraints
                            For embedded deployment, feasibility requires not only speed but also an explicit integrity envelope. The Digital
                              Factor of Safety introduced in Section 2.6 (Eq. 11) expresses the headroom between a measured operating point
                         and a task-defined collapse threshold. Complementarily, Intelligence-per-Watt (IPW) (Eq. 12) operationalizes
                                integrity-aware functional throughput per watt using measured power and a quantified integrity term. Under the
                          measured logs, GPTQ-INT4 improves feasibility jointly in footprint, throughput, and power while remaining
                                inside a high-retention regime (Table 11), which supports IPW as a practical reporting and acceptance objective
                           under strict thermal limits27,28,39,40.
                              At the same time, neither FoSdig nor IPW should be interpreted as universal constants of model quality.
                              Their value lies in deployment assessment: they help determine whether a low-precision operating point is
                            merely compact, or genuinely admissible under a given task, risk tolerance, and power envelope.

                        Operational implication for premier-class motorcycle edge windows
                               Premier-class motorcycles are highly instrumented and support high-rate ECU/DAQ analysis5, yet live telemetry
                                        is restricted in many competitive contexts6. This concentrates actionable intelligence into two practical windows:
                                        (i) local edge interpretation close to the vehicle, and (ii) short garage bursts immediately after a run. Within this
                               envelope, quantization is enabling: it moves an LLM-based semantic layer into a more embedded-feasible region
                           by jointly reducing memory traffic and power while maintaining an explicit integrity margin.
                                     Crucially, the LLM remains an advisory semantic layer for  triage, explanation, summarization, and
                              engineering reasoning. Deterministic stability, actuation, and inner-loop safety remain in the ECU/control stack.
                              This separation bounds the claim of the paper and clarifies where low-precision LLMs are operationally useful.

                      Hardware implications: toward FP4-class “materials”
                        As automotive and edge accelerators adopt native low-precision formats such as INT4 and FP4-class arithmetic23,
                              the framework developed here suggests a certification-style interpretation: precision formats can be treated as
                             computational materials, and deployment should be accepted only when an explicit integrity margin (e.g.,
                            FoSdig) and an energy objective (IPW) are both satisfied.
                                 This shifts the practical question from “Can we run FP4?” to “Under what integrity margins, workload
                               conditions, and runtime settings is FP4-class deployment admissible?” That reframing is especially relevant for
                            edge environments in which memory movement, thermal behavior, and human interaction latency matter as
                      much as nominal arithmetic capability.

                          Limitations and threats to validity
                               Several limitations bound interpretation.
                                         First, the digital operating points characterize inference integrity and efficiency, but do not yet establish
                            end-to-end decision utility across realistic workload mixes; future work should quantify task value under burst
                                constraints and broader telemetry scenarios.

                               Second, throughput and power depend on kernel choice, batch regime, context length, KV-cache policy,
                         and memory hierarchy; reported gains should therefore be interpreted as operating-point evidence under stated
                                     settings, not as universal constants9,20.
                                   Third, perplexity is a sensitive integrity signal but not a domain task. TEE-20 improves construct validity by
                            adding task-grounded reasoning evidence, but it remains a synthetic benchmark rather than a hardware-in-the-
                            loop or on-bike validation.
                                 Fourth, the structural branch is grounded in a concrete computational case study of a Formula Student
                                upright, but it remains a single structural example. It therefore supports the proposed cross-domain framework
                                   at the level of a representative case, not as a universal statement about all motorsport components or topology-
                              optimization workflows.
                                       Fifth, the structural branch is not accompanied here by a new manufactured-part campaign or physical
                          bench validation. It should therefore be interpreted as a computational structural-design study rather than as a
                                      full experimental mechanics validation.
                                       Finally, the present work does not include closed-loop HIL or on-bike testing. The manuscript should
                                therefore be interpreted as a systems-engineering deployment-feasibility study with measured edge operating
                                 points, not as a full experimental validation of an integrated race-vehicle intelligence stack.

                        Future work

                                    1. Telemetry-grounded task validation: evaluate quantized agents on motorsport tasks such as chatter detec­
                                      tion, brake-fade diagnosis, setup-delta explanation, and constraint-aware advisories under explicit edge-win­
                         dow latency budgets and integrity thresholds.
                                    2.  Track-side energy accounting: report Joules/token and IPW with repeated-run confidence intervals for
                               each operating point, enabling more rigorous and comparable efficiency reporting27,28.
                                    3.  Kernel-aware co-design: jointly select PTQ scheme and serving kernel for the target SoC memory hierarchy
                            and batch-1 decode regime; report the full inference stack, including runtime kernel, KV-cache strategy, and
                                  context length8,9,20.
                                    4.  Sensitivity-threshold calibration: calibrate κ and task-specific collapse boundaries using domain bench­
                             marks broader than perplexity, so that FoSdig can evolve from a conservative reporting metric into a bet­
                                ter-grounded deployment criterion.
                                    5. Cross-geometry structural validation: extend the structural branch to additional motorsport components
                            and boundary conditions so that the generalizability of the sensitivity-guided design logic can be assessed
                           more systematically across structural families.
                                    6. Hybrid routing under restricted links: deploy a local INT4 agent for routine interpretation and invoke
                                     frontier or cloud models only for exceptional reasoning, enforcing bounded energy and predictable latency
                              under intermittent connectivity41,42.
                                    7.  Closed-loop and HIL extensions: evaluate whether supervisory semantic assistance can be integrated safely
                                   into track-side engineering workflows with hardware-in-the-loop infrastructure, without encroaching on
                                   deterministic inner-loop control.

                             In premier-class motorsport, “adding lightness” is a prerequisite for feasibility. The same axiom can be extended

                                 assistance more viable under binding memory and thermal constraints.
