# Honda and Yamaha pit electric MX bikes head-to-head – enduros to come?

Published on October 5th, 2026 by Nye Davis

**Two of the biggest names in motorcycling have put their battery research to the test, live, showcasing the fruits of their R&D at this weekend's 2026 Motocross of Nations in Ernée, France – and where MX leads, enduro will surely follow?**

Honda and Yamaha, the largest and third-largest bike manufacturers globally, respectively, utilised the biggest event on the MX calendar to pit their leccy bikes head-to-head in two special demos, held Saturday and Sunday. It also marks the first time we've really seen the Yam ridden in anger since its reveal as a concept at EICMA last year.

In front of the massive crowds, Honda brought its CR Electric Proto, ridden by Supercross ace Justin Brayton and Trey Canard, who was Honda's first rider to compete on an electric motocross bike in the All Japan Motocross Championship in 2023.

The big H's electric dirt bike is not exactly new, having been fielded in the 2024 FIM E-Xplorer World Cup with Team HRC. There, Honda won the Men's Championship in its first season in the series, while finishing second in both the Women's Championship and Team Championship.

This weekend has marked the first public running of the Yamaha though, which first appeared at EICMA in Milan last year. It was ridden by 2004 MX2 World Champion and 2007 AMA Supercross 250cc East Champion Ben Townley, alongside 2011 MX3 World Championship runner-up and three-time Enduropale du Touquet winner Milko Potisek.

The YE-01 is Yamaha's take on electric motocross, blending a YZ450F chassis with a powertrain developed by French trials specialists Electric Motion (EM).

All well and good. But here's the bit we're interested in.

**So, are electric enduro bikes coming next?**

There's nothing concrete to suggest either Honda or Yamaha is about to launch an electric enduro bike. In fact, there's not even a sliver of evidence publicly available to suggest either firm is even working on one. But given both have cracked the MX and trials game with battery power – albeit neither offering bikes you or I can actually buy – it marks the obvious next step.

We've received zero teasers from either ahead of EICMA 2026, so we can probably safely rule out the possibility of one suddenly appearing in Milan next month. Nonetheless, Enduro21 will be on the ground at the show and will put the question to those in the know.

The market is not exactly short of petrol-free enduro bikes, there seems to be an ever-growing list of them popping up from new startups. Like it or not, it seems the tech is growing quickly and is here to stay.

That said, it's a market currently dominated by names that most of us would never have heard of a few years ago. Heck, the wonder kids at Stark Future have been around publicly for less than five years...

What we don't have, at least not yet, is a full-sized battery bike from one of the mainstream Japanese or European manufacturers (KTM's Freeride E doesn't count!). So come on major manufacturers, what are you playing at? When the a mainstream industry gonna get in on the action?

**The Yamaha has the missing ingredient**

Between the red and the blue camp, the Yamaha is perhaps the more interesting candidate for a bit of enduro-fication, down to one big reason – it has a proper clutch.

Its powertrain was developed by Electric Motion, and EM uses hydraulically-operated diaphragm clutches on the majority of its electric trials models, as well as its trials-cum-enduro mashup that is the Escape XR (which again doesn't really count because it's essentially a trials bike with a seat, bigger battery and USD fork fitted).

All this leaves the door open for Yamaha's engineers to pull their fingers out and turn the YE-01 into something with a headlight. Whether Yamaha actually does that is another matter. We'll be asking.

**Meanwhile, Belgium wins the MXon**

Away from the electric-bike demos, Belgium ended a 13-year wait for victory in motocross's biggest team event at Ernée.

Liam Everts and Lucas Coenen delivered when it mattered across the three races to secure Belgium's first Motocross of Nations triumph since 2013. For Great Britain, Ben Watson produced two strong rides as Team GB finished seventh overall, while the individual race victories went to Romain Febvre and Jeffrey Herlings, with the Dutchman winning both the second and third motos.
