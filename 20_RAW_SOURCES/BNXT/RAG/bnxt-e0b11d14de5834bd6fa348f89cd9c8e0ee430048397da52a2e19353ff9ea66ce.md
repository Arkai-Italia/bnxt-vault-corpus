# Tested: Ducati Desmo250 MX – Friendly, fast, and mightily impressive

Published on September 5th, 2026 by Nye Davis

#### **We're enduro front and centre here, but when the good folks at Ducati got in touch offering a go on their fresh-faced Desmo250 MX, the answer was a resounding yes. So, is it any good? And should we all be eagerly awaiting the Desmo250 EDS that's set to arrive in the next year or two?**

Let's get one thing out the way first and foremost – I'm not a motocross racer, and the odd test aside, I haven't been to an MX track in over a decade. Tony Cairoli I am most definitely not.

Give me roots, rocks, and hills and I'm happy. Testament to the little Ducati, though, because right from the off, around our Monte Coralli circuit testbed, the bike immediately proved intuitive, predictable, and superbly capable. Here's hoping the forthcoming EDS model is equally impressive.

### **First impressions**

An initial nose around the bike reveals a product clearly screwed together with a great deal of care. Quality is top notch and on a par with anything else coming out of Europe or Japan. Italy's biggest motorcycle manufacturer is clearly taking this foray into the dirt very seriously and looking to inject a little of the bougie-ness we've come to expect from its road bikes into the new off-road catalogue.

After thumbing the motor into life and departing the pits, it's immediately apparent just how well the motor pulls from the midrange right up to that bonkers 15,000rpm limiter. This bike loves to rev. Hold it wide open and it laps it up.

Chatting to a few moto-specific journos, the consensus was that it lacks ever-so-slightly the hit of some models, but the linearity and breadth of the power delivery are top notch. If you're after a more explosive out-of-the-crate delivery, the baby Desmo is probably not for you. Then again, the 450 has that covered...

Corners are handled with an almost telepathic precision – simply look for a rut, point the 250 MX at it and keep the throttle open and it seems to do the rest.

Suspension-wise, the bike comes set up on the soft side, which felt right at home coming from squidgy enduro bikes. Even so, while perhaps a spring rate down for my weight, the Showa hardware still takes care of business admirably.

The electronics package, likewise, is mightily impressive. Two maps are on offer as standard, comprising a buttery soft 'green' mode – which also ramps up the Desmo's clever 'real-time' traction control (TC) – and a spicier 'red' map, which offers more bark and less intrusive electronics.

A little bit of app-based fiddling (made possible with the addition of the £177.98 optional Wi-Fi module) allows plenty of tunability.

My personal Goldilocks set-up consisted of power delivery at its most aggressive, minimal engine braking, and the TC on, but turned down to the minimum. I did run a few sessions with the latter off but ultimately found that a bit of electronic intervention made things less fatiguing. On the flip side, with the TC turned up, the bike tends to feel a little boggy on corner exits or while climbing steeper hills.

### **Desmo250 EDS expectations**

Ducati has already confirmed on record that a quarter-litre enduro bike is in the pipeline, though specific details remain thin on the ground at this stage.

Having ridden the 450 EDS the previous day, there's every reason to think the Desmo250 EDS will be a belter when it finally arrives.

It'll no doubt lose some of that sky-high redline, with more reciprocating mass added to the motor to keep things spinning at low revs (the 450 EDS has nearly 30% more crank inertia than its MX sibling), but the 450 EDS has proven Ducati's engineers know their salt when it comes to building an enduro bike. Assuming they follow the same formula, the smaller EDS model looks set to be one to watch when it lands in the next 12-24 months.
