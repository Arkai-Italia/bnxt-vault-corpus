# Electric Motion overhauls Escape R with bigger battery and new motor

Published on September 30th, 2026 by Nye Davis

#### Electric Motion (EM) has lifted the lid on its latest generation of battery-powered trials bikes, among which is the do-it-all Escape R, now packing a bigger battery, more power, a revised chassis and posh new TFT dash.

The French firm took the 2026 Trial des Nations in Arteixo, Spain, as the site for the big reveal of its latest stable.

A quick whiz through the spec sheet shows the new Escape R now packs a 3.8kWh battery – up from 3.5 last year. The enlarged power source is now in line with the bike’s more enduro-ey Escape XR sister model. EM hasn’t announced any updates for that model for the new year, so we’ll stand by for further news. As it’s pretty much the only electric enduro bike with a proper clutch on the market, let’s hope it sticks around though. This latest Escape R model also retains the hydraulic diaphragm clutch set-up.

Other numbers include a new 18kW motor – that's a smidge over 24bhp – which spins up to 14,000rpm. Torque is quoted at 740Nm or 545.80 ft-lbs, which sounds utterly mad as leccy bike figures always do, but that’s a rear wheel figure massively amplified by gear reduction, not motor output as is given with ICE machines. This little 87kg trials bike-with-a-seat is not actually outputting 10 times the torque of a 450...

EM has also increased motor inertia by 40 percent, aiming to give the Escape R a smoother and more progressive response at low speed.

The motor’s character can be adjusted through different power maps, while the flywheel can be fitted with different weights to further alter its response.

Backing it up is the aforementioned new 3800Wh battery developed by EM’s own R&D department – who also have a hand in Yamaha’s electric off-road powertrain work.

The 50.4V pack weighs 20kg and is claimed to offer up to four hours of riding, although actual range will obviously depend heavily on how hard you’re riding it.

Charging takes around 4h 20min with the standard 12A charger, while EM’s optional 25A unit cuts that to approximately 2h 30min.

### **Chassis rethink and electronic add-ons**

EM has also fettled the R’s chassis and fitted a new swingarm, which they say improves rigidity and precision. Suspension comes from TECH, with a DLC-treated steel fork offering 175mm of travel and an R16 V rear shock providing 170mm.

FRB (Fixed Regenerative Brake) continues to feature, which provides regenerative braking and recovers energy under deceleration. The optional plug-and-play PRB R system is available for riders wanting greater control of the reg (and what is effectively a hand-operated rear brake)

There’s also TKO, which maintains a constant motor speed to help with traction and low-speed control, while Anti-Reverse prevents the bike from rolling backwards on steep climbs and releases when the throttle is opened.

For 2027, those systems are displayed through a new TFT dash showing speed, motor rpm, remaining range, riding map and TKO status.

The Escape R can also be linked to EM Connect, allowing riders to adjust settings including power delivery, motor response and TKO, as well as monitor the bike and activate its locking system.

The new Escape R 2027 is priced at €10,290 including VAT in Europe and homologated for road use as equivalent to a 125cc machine.
