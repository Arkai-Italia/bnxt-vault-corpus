# Kove's new 350 Rally could unlock the world's biggest bike market

Published on August 16th, 2026 by Nye Davis

**A new, slimmed-down 'rally' bike could soon be on the way from Kove, as newly surfaced patent drawings reveal a new 350 in the works from the Chinese manufacturer. And while a more affordable smaller-bore version of the popular 450 Rally would undoubtedly have some appeal to European and North American audiences, the real significance of the bike may lie much further afield.**

If Kove is serious about becoming a global player, a 350 could be the key to cracking the world's biggest motorcycle market - India.

The documents filed show a machine broadly similar to the 450, only with a smaller motor and a few subtle tweaks here and there.

The frame, swingarm and distinctive low-slung fuel tank arrangement all look familiar, though we'd expect some parts (particularly the forks and shock) to be lower spec to contribute towards a lower overall asking price.

The engine is clearly different. Patent illustrations appear to show a parallel twin, raising the possibility that Kove could be adapting the motor from its existing 350R roadster, which produces around 47bhp in current trim.

If that's the case, the final package would naturally be considerably less focused than the race-derived 450, adding a dash of mass-market appeal and road going civility – anyone that's ridden the 450 will attest that an around town commuter it is not!

Some observers have suggested the bike is aimed at European A2 licence holders, and that certainly makes sense given the power output. However, to us at least, the biggest opportunity surely lies on a different continent altogether.

India is comfortably the world's largest powered two-wheeler market, with annual motorcycle sales measuring around twenty times those of the combined top five European markets. Kove doesn't currently sell motorcycles there, but a 350 Rally could represent a logical first step in joining the ever-growing market.

Recent changes to India's Goods and Services Tax (GST) have created a clear divide between motorcycles below and above 350cc.

Bikes below that threshold have recently experienced a tax reduction, falling from 28% to 18%, making smaller-cc models far more affordable. Meanwhile, larger-capacity machines now attract a whopping 40% flat rate, meaning entry with the existing 450 would put Kove at a significant disadvantage from the off - whereas as a fresh faced 349cc option would do just nicely.

As ever with patent filings, nothing guarantees production, and Kove has yet to confirm the bike officially.
