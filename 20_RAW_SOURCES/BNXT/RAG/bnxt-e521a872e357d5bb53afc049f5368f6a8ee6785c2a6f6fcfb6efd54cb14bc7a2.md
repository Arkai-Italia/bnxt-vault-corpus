# A work order instead of a notebook

“We never ordered that” is the most expensive phrase in a workshop. Here the work order is built in the app, the client **approves the scope and the price before the work starts**, and finished jobs land in the maintenance log of their bike automatically.

BEFOREand after

**Before**The work order is a verbal agreement and a notebook. At pickup, an argument: “we didn't ask for that, that's too expensive.”

**After**A work order with itemized jobs and a total the system calculates. The client approved it in the app — the scope is locked. Any edit after approval returns the order to estimate status.

**Before**The client's bike history lives in the head of a mechanic who quit last season.

**After**On request, the client opens their maintenance log to you: what was done, when, at what engine hours. Diagnosis starts from facts, not from questioning.

**Before**The client rides off — and disappears. Whether they come back for the next service depends on their memory.

**After**Your work is recorded in their garage, the next service countdown ticks by engine hours — and leads them back to you.

TOOLKITin the app

### Itemized work orders

Jobs and parts as line items, totals calculated by the server. Your standard price list fills in automatically — an order takes minutes to build.

### Client approval

The estimate goes to the client in the app. Work starts after their confirmation — price disputes are ruled out by design.

### Workshop roles

Owner, service advisors, mechanics — each with their own access. Work orders don't get lost between shifts.

### The client's maintenance log

On-request access to the service history and engine hours of their bike. A completed work order closes items on the maintenance schedule automatically.

### Requests from riders

A bike owner sends a service request straight from their garage — to their club's mechanics or to your workshop.
