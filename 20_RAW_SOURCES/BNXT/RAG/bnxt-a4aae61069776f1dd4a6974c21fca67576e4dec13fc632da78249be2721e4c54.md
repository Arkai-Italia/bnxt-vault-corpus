# Tested: SR75 Suzuki Off-Road Experience Centre

Published on September 17th, 2026 by Nye Davis

-2.jpg)

**Enduro21 heads to the Valleys to try out the recently opened SR75 Off-Road Experience Centre, sampling Suzuki's trail and adventure toy chest and maybe learning a thing or two along the way, too.**

There are worse ways to spend a Thursday than playing with motorbikes in the hills of South Wales, better still if doing so comes with tuition and without the need to wash your bike after. So, when Suzuki invited Enduro21 along for a spot of serious journalisting to review the place (okay, it was really just a jolly), I was more than happy to take one for the team and head along.

### **What is it**

Suzuki's own blurb describes the school as a place for 'riders of all levels and ability', set among the landscape overlooking Merthyr Tydfil.

Here, you'll find (again, according to Suzuki) some 300 acres of "trails of varying complexity, from wide, flat, shale fire roads to more technical, single-track trails."

"Instruction will focus on everything from the basics of body positioning and throttle control and braking to more advanced skills and how to tackle more challenging terrain," the release continues.

Group sizes are kept to a maximum of three trainees per instructor, which is obviously handy if you're going with the intention of learning, rather than just taking an extended unpaved test ride for the day.

Unlike some other centres, bookings are not determined by ability – e.g. you cannot book on to a level one course as a total beginner or a level three session as someone more experienced. That feels like a bit of a miss to us and, naturally, lumping an unknown group together of potentially very mismatched abilities does leave room for problems to arise. Not a complaint, just food for thought, Suzuki.

In any group of riders, you are bound to have mixed levels of experience, and on the press day we attended, the ability spectrum was indeed pretty wide. But thanks to some can-do spirit all-round and a willingness to keep going after a tumble or two, everyone had a cracking day out (we certainly did).

### **What to expect**

On the day, the learning begins right from the off and starts from the dead basics to bring everyone up to a safe level. Before even turning a wheel, first things first is a lesson in body position for dummies by chief instructor Geoff Walker, who boasts over 30 years of experience when it comes to teaching people how to ride off-road.

With everyone squared away with the fundamentals of the attack position, and how to deal with braking and acceleration forces while standing, next up is becoming familiar with using the clutch. It may sound a little silly if you've got years of enduro under your belt, but bear in mind that the school is primarily aimed at road-riding folk whose clutch-control experience extends only to pulling away then stopping at the next junction. If you're a first-timer to the dirt, there's nothing to worry about here – Geoff and the gang have you covered with a few simple drills to get you up to speed.

From there, it's off into the wild (well, not quite), and after a session of slow-speed follow-the-leader work and some figure eights, the riding progressively becomes more adventurous, with instruction given along the way. This included lots of wide-open fire road-type stuff, singletrack, plenty of puddles (it is Wales, after all) and a couple of laps around the onsite motocross track – who knew DRZs were so good in the air?

By the end of our day, our group had people with minimal time off-tarmac beforehand sending (we use that term loosely) 230kg V-Strom 800 DEs up singletrack hill climbs – thanks in part to their own tenacity, as well as the help and encouragement of the instructors.

### **The bikes**

That brings me on nicely to the machines available to take out. Chances are, if you're thinking about booking a spot at the school, it's because you a) quite fancy learning to ride off-road but don't want to spanner yourself or your pride and joy in the middle of nowhere by yourself (understandable) and b) would like to do so on a Suzuki because you are thinking of, or already have, purchased one of the manufacturer's adventure or trail bikes.

At the centre, you're given the choice between a V-Strom 800DE, V-Strom 1050DE (though not when we were there) and a [DR-Z4S](https://enduro21.com/en/news/tested-suzuki-dr-z4s-ticking-the-old-school-trail-bike-boxes). You're not bound to the bike you choose in the morning, and there's plenty of stock available for swapping around. I started the day on the little DR before having a go on the Strom after lunch.

Facilities-wise, the SR75 site is situated atop a pretty sizable hill, set among lots of other pretty sizeable hills. Therefore, amenities are basic but ample for what you need. There's a toilet, changing/storage room, hot drinks aplenty, drinking water and snacks. What else could you really need? Merthyr Tydfil is only a stone's throw away, too, should you wish to stay the night or fancy a meal out afterwards.

### **Who's it for and what's included**

As mentioned above, the training days are for everyone, and we can all benefit from a little friendly critiquing. Largely because of the mixed group structure, the experience is perhaps best for those who are new, or at least fairly new, to riding big bikes off-road. That said, I had a blast, as did others on the day who’re accustomed to riding in the dirt, and there are plenty of opportunities to throw in a few skids and wheelies into the group ride, provided you don't take the mick or do anything that could potentially detract from the fun other guests are having - or put anyone in danger, obviously.

Bookings start at £300 per person. For your cash you get bike hire, all fuel and maintenance, refreshments, on-hand instruction and, the best bit, no need to wash the machine which you've spent the past five hours getting filthy.

For more information and to book, visit: [www.sr75offroad.co.uk](https://www.sr75offroad.co.uk/)
